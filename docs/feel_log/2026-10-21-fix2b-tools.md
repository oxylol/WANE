## Why

**A3, a crack in every joint.** `shapes.build` decides whether a cut piece of an outline is on the silhouette by testing its midpoint against every other piece with the winding rule, which is half-open: a point on a left or top edge is inside, on a right or bottom edge outside. Where two parts share an edge exactly, each edge's midpoint lies on the other's outline, so one edge counted as covered and the other did not, and the one that did not stayed in the silhouette set. S there read -0.5 u, so the shader drew a rim and a line across the inside of a shape. Ash's rig meets at joint lines (task 2), so this would have been the first thing seen on her. Now a midpoint within 1e-6 u of another piece's outline is found by distance (`sdf.near_outline`) and decided by which side the other piece lies on: across the segment, the parts abut and the seam is interior; on the segment's own side, both outlines are the union's and stay, and a front part hides the back part's copy of the same line from the visible set. The seam is still drawn once, as the front part's visible edge (U 0.375 on both sides of x = 22 at 4x, `side` 255 inside the front part, 0 beyond it). The cuts take an end that touches to rounding, which a rotated part needs: a 90 degree turn leaves coordinates 1e-15 off, and without it the long edge of a T junction was not split at the short part's corner and the exposed stretches were lost with the shared one. 14 tests in `tools/artbuild/tests/test_shared_edges.py` fail on the old code (all 14, run before the fix) and pass on the new: abutting on both axes in both z orders, a 3 x 3 tile grid, T junctions on a vertical and a horizontal edge, a part turned by an SVG transform, by a pose, by the other three quarter turns, both parts turned together by 37 degrees, a group turned 30 degrees, and same-sided edges (B in A's corner, top and bottom). Each compares S to the signed distance to the union polygon, written out by hand in plain numpy, within one quantisation step. `near_outline` has its own five tests. The committed sheets are byte-for-byte what they were (`build.py --check` ok), so no asset in the repository had the crack.

**A10, the sign check looked at two folders.** The cairosvg cross-check (S's sign against cairo on every firm pixel, at 1x, 2x and 4x) and the lint over the shipped sources looped over `(PROBE_DIR, CANDLE_DIR)`. `lit_test_room` and `crawler` were never checked, and nothing told whoever added a fourth. `common.part_libraries()` walks for `parts.svg` and raises if there is none. The two unchecked libraries pass (28 s for the whole class). A test holds every library found to a root of `build.py`.

**F9, the tool that drew a world that was not there.** The first fix round's `room01_*.png` were committed as an empty world because `--room zone1/01` is not a room id (`zone1/01_dash_slash` is) and `render_room.gd` ignored `Simulation.load_errors`. Reproduced on the old code: exit 0, two pictures, 4.0 s. Now `render_room.gd` stops with exit 1 before it draws and says what is wrong (no room file at that path, no room named, a different room, no player), exit 2 for a usage mistake, 1 for a fixture that does not parse; `render.sh`'s exit status was also swallowed by its noise filter (`... | grep -v ... || true`) and is now the script's. `ci/lighting.sh` holds all of it. The two empty pictures are deleted from `2026-10-21-art-pipeline-fixes/zone1/` (the entry says so).

**I11.** `ci/perf.sh` called what the frame bench runs "the Pitch lighting stand-in"; it runs `LightField` at Pitch. The code map's `game/lighting/` list now has `LightSurfaces` and `WaneLight`; to stay under 12,000 characters one sentence of the last rules line that `CLAUDE.md` already says (the doc wins over code unless an ADR says otherwise) was dropped, and two phrasings were shortened. Every path is kept.

**Room 03, pogo chain.** Dusk: three pale one-way lines (97) and rows of red teeth (65 to 71, the brightest saturated thing in the frame) over lilac floors (48). **Room 05, wall-kick climb.** Dusk: a tall shaft, brown smooth blocks (53.5) and one lilac strip, a tile wide, down the shaft's right side (50.8): the clingable wall. **Room 09, reverse pogo.** Dusk: a steel-blue bell (75) with a white strike mark on top (234) on a lilac floor. **Room 12, kindle dash.** Dusk: two amber wax screens (84) against air 15, and a spike pit between them (66).
