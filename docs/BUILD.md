# WANE — Build Document

Oct 16, 2026 · @Filip

## 1. Vision & Pillars

**WANE** is a hand-drawn 2D action roguelike. You are a Wick: a pilgrim carrying the last ember of a swallowed sun, descending through a dead world to relight it. The dark rises faster the longer you take, so every run is a wager between greed and speed.

It takes the precision melee platforming and painterly melancholy of Hollow Knight and runs it through the time-pressure, stacking items and escalating swarms of Risk of Rain Returns. Neither game is copied; both are studied. Every name, creature, system and line of lore here is original.

**Codename:** Project Wane · **Genre:** 2D action roguelike platformer · **Players:** 1 (co-op is a stretch goal, designed for from day one) · **Run length:** 35 to 60 minutes · **Platforms:** PC and Steam Deck first (Steam); consoles later through a porting partner · **Engine:** Godot 4.x (rationale in section 13)

**Who builds it:** Claude Code authors everything: code, tools, art sources and renders, animation, score and sound, writing, tests and builds. The director (you) plays every milestone, judges feel, approves gates and answers the questions the doc leaves open. There is no art team, no composer and no outsourcing; the quality bar does not move because of that.

### The four pillars

Everything in the game is tested against these four sentences. If a feature weakens one, it is cut.

1. **Movement is the character.** Every frame of input is honored. The level is a playground before it is a gauntlet. Speed is defense. If the player ever feels the game took control from them, that is a bug with P0 priority.
2. **Combat is a conversation.** Melee-first, directional, cancelable and readable. Enemies telegraph, the player answers, the answer has weight. Items change what your hits *do*; they never change how your hits *feel*.
3. **Light is the picture.** Every shape on screen is authored from source (vector, shader, procedure), never stock, never generated by a raster model. The one true dynamic light in the world is the ember you carry. Beauty is not decoration here; it is information, and it is a mechanic.
4. **The dark rises.** Time is the enemy. The Wane Clock turns the world against you minute by minute. Decisions are about tempo, and the game never lets you forget it.

### The GOTY bar

"Spare no expense" is a measurable standard, not a mood. The game ships only when all of these hold:

| Measure | Target |
| --- | --- |
| Input-to-photon latency | ≤ 2 frames at 60 Hz (33 ms) on the reference PC; ≤ 3 frames on consoles |
| Frame rate | Locked 60 on consoles and Steam Deck; unlocked to 240 on PC, with deterministic 60 Hz simulation |
| Frame-time variance | 99th percentile under 1.5 ms of jitter in combat |
| Art | Every sprite, background, VFX and UI element authored by Claude from vector, shader and procedural source; no stock assets, no raster image generators, zero placeholder art at release |
| Animation | Player at 24 fps from authored key poses with generated smears; enemies at 12 to 24 fps; no linear-tween characters |
| Movement | All 12 core tech interactions (section 5) stable and taught in-game |
| Content | 6 zones, 7 Wicks, 120+ Votives, 60+ enemies, 12 bosses, 3 endings |
| Feel QA | Section 15 checklist passes on every milestone build with zero exceptions |
| Accessibility | Full remapping, 4 assist toggles, screen-shake and flash controls, colorblind-safe telegraphs |

The reference for quality is not "good for an indie". It is: a stranger watching a run on a stream cannot tell whether the player or the game is doing the beautiful thing.

## 2. The World of the Wane

The sun was swallowed. What fell to the bottom of the world was not a sun but the thing that ate it, and it is still hungry. That is the lie the Wicks have never been told, and the truth the player uncovers across many descents.

### The swallowing

There was a sun. The old tongue calls it the **Hearth**. For an age it hung over a world of moth-folk, wax-folk and the deep tide, and it never set; night was a story told to frighten larvae.

Then something came out of the far dark between stars: the **Hush**. It did not attack. It simply opened, and the Hearth went in. The sky dimmed to a bruise and stayed that way. The age that followed is the **Wane**: a long twilight in which the world is slowly cooling, and everything that lives is slowly forgetting what warmth was.

The Hush, full, sank. It fell through the sky, through the surface and through the roots of the world, and came to rest in the deepest chamber of all, a place now called the **Gutter**. There it sleeps and digests. The light it swallowed leaks from it in a thin, red glow. From far above, the moth-folk saw that glow and called it the **Ember**, and they believed it was the Hearth, fallen and waiting to be carried home.

### The Wicks

Some moth-folk are born with a sliver of the old light in their chest. It burns visibly through the thorax; it keeps them warm; it makes them targets. They are called **Wicks**. The Vigil, the old knightly order, raised them as pilgrims with a single purpose: descend to the Ember, offer the sliver, and relight the sun.

No Wick has ever come back. The Vigil said this was because they succeeded and ascended into the restored sun. The Vigil was wrong. Every Wick who reached the Gutter fed the Hush, and every feeding made it hungrier, and every hungry year the dark rose a little faster.

The player is the last Wick. There are no more slivers. What the player does at the bottom is the ending.

### The Snuff

When a moth-folk's inner warmth goes out, they do not die. They become **Snuffed**: hollow, cold, moving by old habit, drawn toward any light they can find and consuming it. Most enemies in the game are Snuffed, or things that have adapted to feed on the Snuffed. The dark that rises during a run is not weather. It is the Hush breathing out, and every exhale Snuffs a little more of the world.

### The factions, and what became of them

| Faction | Who they were | Where they are now |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The Vigil** | Moth-knights who kept the watch at the Hearth's old temples and trained the Wicks. Devout, armored in beaten lantern-brass. | Their fortress-cathedral stands in zone 3. Most are Snuffed inside their armor. Their last Knight-Abbess, Serah Candlewright, is a boss and, later, an ally. |
| **The Tallowborn** | Wax-folk who built the great dripping cities under the surface. Engineers, chandlers, hoarders of heat. | Their warrens (zone 2) are half-melted. The living ones trade with Wicks; the Snuffed ones became the Tallowmen. |
| **The Choir** | A deep-sea people who sang to keep the tide in place. Their song was the world's second light; you could navigate by it. | The sea came in when they stopped singing (zone 4). Some still sing, wrongly, and the wrong song is a weapon. |
| **The Lamplighters** | A secret third order inside the Vigil who suspected the truth about the Ember. | Hunted as heretics. Their hidden notes, found across runs, are how the player learns what the Ember is. |
| **The Hush** | Not a faction. The thing at the bottom. | Dreaming. Digesting. Waiting for the last sliver. |

### The three endings

- **The Offering.** Give the sliver to the Ember. The sun does not return. The screen goes dark, and the credits roll in near-silence. This is the default ending, and it is a trap the game lets the player walk into once.
- **The Snuffing.** Having found the Lamplighter notes, refuse the offering and fight the Hush itself, using the sliver as a weapon. Kill it, and the swallowed light bursts out of its corpse: a sunrise, after an age. The world will be warm again, and the player will not be alive to see it.
- **The Lantern.** Having rescued the Knight-Abbess and the last of the Choir across multiple runs, bring both to the Gutter. The Choir sings the true song, the Abbess holds the Hush open, and the player walks into it carrying the sliver, and walks out the other side carrying the Hearth. The sun rises with a person still inside it. Three-run commitment; the game's true ending.

### Lore delivery rules

- Lore is never a wall of text. It lives in environment, enemy design, item descriptions, NPC dialogue (short, in-character) and 40 Lamplighter notes found in the world.
- A player who skips every note still understands the story through the art: zone 1 is twilight, zone 6 is pitch black lit only by the red Ember, and the Ember's chamber is visibly shaped like a stomach.
- Every item description is two lines: one mechanical, one in-world. The in-world line is written by a character (a chandler, a knight, a heretic), and tone shifts by author.
- Nothing in the lore explains a mechanic. The Wane Clock is not justified; it is felt.

## 3. The Descent

A run is six zones deep, each a hand-built level stitched from authored rooms, ending in a Beacon, a Vigil and a boss. The player chooses between two zone variants at each depth, so a full run sees 6 of 12 possible levels.

### Run loop

1. **Arrive.** Drop into the zone. The Wane Clock is running. The player's ember lights a radius around them; the zone's ambient light shrinks as the clock advances.
2. **Explore.** Find Votives (items) in sealed Tapers (chests, paid for with Tallow, the run currency), Shrines, enemy drops and hidden rooms reachable only with movement tech. Every room is designed to reward a movement skill with a shortcut or a secret.
3. **Light the Beacon.** A dormant lantern-tower somewhere in the zone. Lighting it starts the **Vigil**: a 90-second hold (zones 1 to 2) rising to 150 seconds (zones 5 to 6) in which the Beacon's light radius shrinks unless the player kills enemies inside it. The dark pours toward the light. Kill count refills the radius; standing outside the radius drains the player's Flame.
4. **Boss.** The Vigil ends when the Beacon reaches full brightness; the boss arrives through the light. Bosses are zone-specific, chosen from a pool of two per zone.
5. **Descend.** The Beacon opens the way down. Lingering is allowed; the clock does not care.
6. **The Gutter.** The sixth zone is a single long vertical fall through a shaft of total darkness lit only by the Ember below, then the final chamber.

### Branching and pacing

- The zone choice at each depth is shown on the Beacon at the end of the previous zone, with a one-line description and the alt's modifier (e.g. "Pilgrim Road: long, fast, fewer Tapers").
- Zone size scales down with depth: zone 1 is 60 rooms, zone 5 is 35. Early zones teach; late zones test.
- Target zone time at skilled play: 6 minutes for zones 1 and 2, 8 for 3 and 4, 10 for 5. A greedy player can double that, and will pay for it.
- Each zone has 3 hidden rooms reachable only with specific tech (a pogo chain, a wall-cling extension, a kindle-dash through a wax wall). Hidden rooms hold a guaranteed Lantern-tier Votive or a Lamplighter note.
- A **Shrine of the Lamplighters** appears once per run in a random zone. It offers one Soot item (section 8) and one note.

## 4. The Wicks

Seven playable Wicks, each a different movement kit before it is a different combat kit. Every Wick shares the universal base (run, jump, dash, wall-cling, pogo) and overrides two or three verbs; the shared base keeps muscle memory, the overrides make each one feel like a new game.

### Shared design rules

- Every Wick has exactly 4 abilities on 4 buttons plus Mend. No ability has more than 2 inputs. Nothing is on a menu.
- Every Wick can clear every zone's hidden rooms with their own kit; the tech differs, the access does not.
- Every Wick has a **Signature**: one animation, one sound and one VFX that plays when they do the thing their kit is built for (Vesper vaulting over a boss, Cantor chaining 4 echo-steps). The Signature is the shot in the trailer.
- Base stats differ by ±15% at most. A Wick is never "the tanky one"; a Wick is the one that moves this way.
- Each Wick has a 20-line character arc told through Hearth dialogue and a unique final conversation with the Hush.

### Feel notes per Wick

- **Ash** should feel like a scalpel. Light, exact, no waste. The reference kit, so it gets the most tuning time.
- **Vesper** should feel like a thrown spear: slow to wind, unstoppable once committed. Her vault is the single most satisfying move in the game or she is cut.
- **Moth** should feel like falling with grace. Glide must be floaty but never mushy; the wing-flap has a hard snap.
- **Tallow** should feel like a bowling ball that laughs. Slides have real speed; the Stamp lands with the heaviest hitstop in the game.
- **Cantor** should feel like playing an instrument. The note has a pitch tied to aim direction; chaining echo-steps plays a melody.
- **Pyre** should feel reckless. The screen should be a mess of fire by zone 3 and the player should feel like the cause.
- **The Hollow** should feel like the game with the training wheels off: no safety nets, inhuman speed, the sound mix with the music ducked out.

## 5. Movement Specification

The simulation runs at a fixed 60 Hz; all numbers below are in frames (f) at 60 and in units where 1 unit = 1 world pixel at the native 1920×1080 art scale. The player collider is 20 units wide and 44 units tall. These are starting values for Ash, the reference Wick; they are tuned by feel, and the tuned values are the spec.

### Ground movement

| Property | Value | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Max run speed | 540 u/s (9 u/f) | About 3.5 body-widths per second |
| Ground acceleration | 0 to max in 6 f | Snappy; no "skating" |
| Ground deceleration | max to 0 in 4 f | Stops on a coin |
| Turnaround | 2 f, keeps 60% speed in the new direction | Never a dead frame on reversal |
| Crouch | Hitbox 44 to 28 tall; cannot move; drops through one-way platforms on down+jump | The crouched box cannot move in the air either; crouch and drop-through need the stick straight down (docs/adr/0010) |
| Slope handling | Up to 45°, speed unchanged; no sliding on slopes |  |
| Corner correction | Horizontal: 8 u nudge over ledge corners; vertical: 6 u nudge around ceiling corners | The player never clips a corner they visually cleared; on the ground the 8 u nudge steps the player up onto a lip, such as a door left open as a floor (docs/adr/0008); the collider moves at once and the drawn player eases onto it, from rest over 3 frames, its speed changing by at most a quarter of the nudge a frame, so no nudge (nor a staircase of them) is seen as a hop; it never falls more than twice the largest nudge behind (`content/player_view.tres`, docs/adr/0012) |

| Terminal velocity | 1500 u/s |  |

### Pogo (down-strike)

The pogo is the heart of the movement system. Attacking down while airborne, on hitting an enemy, hazard or pogo-able surface (a bell, a spike, a wax bulb, a candle flame), bounces the player.

| Property | Value | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bounce velocity | 794 u/s | Slightly under a jump (88%; docs/feel_log/2026-10-17-pogo-bounce.md) |
| Momentum | Horizontal speed fully preserved; vertical replaced | A dash-jump into a pogo keeps the dash speed |
| Resets | Double jump, air dash | Pogo chains are infinite with rhythm |
| Hitbox | 36 u wide, 24 u tall, under the player, active frames 3 to 9 of the down-strike | Generous on purpose |
| Chain window | Next down-strike can start on frame 6 after a bounce | Cadence of about 4 bounces per second at its fastest (frame 14 was tried and reverted; the faster pogo-dash skim is a director question: docs/feel_log/2026-10-17-pogo-chain-window.md) |

### The twelve core tech interactions

All of these must work for every Wick (with their overrides) and be taught by the world, never by a tutorial pop-up.

1. **Dash-slash**: dash into attack, frame-perfect cancel. Taught by a tight corridor of weak enemies.
2. **Dash-jump**: carry dash speed into a jump. Taught by a gap exactly one dash-jump wide: since the air jump, the air dash and the Ember Toss carry every route further, that is a width only a route with a dash-jump crosses, 1664 u for Ash over a pit, 62 u past the farthest route without one (docs/adr/0010; `tools/replay/gap_reach.gd` measures it).
3. **Pogo chain**: bounce off a line of enemies or hazards. Taught by a spike pit with Snuffed in it.
4. **Pogo-dash reset**: pogo resets the air dash, so pogo, dash, pogo again crosses anything. Taught by a hidden room.
5. **Wall-kick climb**: climb a single wall with neutral kicks. Taught by a well.
6. **Wall-jump dash**: wall-jump, then dash, then wall-cling the next wall. Taught by a shaft.
7. **Coyote dash**: dash off a ledge within coyote time keeps ground cooldown rules. Taught by a cliff.
8. **Attack-cancel landing**: landing never interrupts an attack. Taught by a boss with a short window.
9. **Reverse pogo**: pogo while moving backward to reposition behind an enemy. Taught by a shielded enemy.
10. **Crouch-dash under**: crouch, then dash, keeps the 28-tall hitbox for the dash. Taught by a low gap.
11. **Ember-toss cancel** (Ash): throw the ember mid-dash, teleport after landing. Taught by a chasm.
12. **Kindle-dash** (universal with the Kindle Votive): dash through a wax wall or a Snuffed to ignite it. Taught by a wax door.

### Feel rules (non-negotiable)

- **Input is read every frame, before physics, and acted on the same frame.** No input ever waits for an animation.
- **Animations serve the state machine, never the reverse.** If a move needs 9 frames, the animation is 9 frames. Smears and holds are used to make 9 frames read as 24.
- **No move has more than 2 frames of startup unless it has armor.** Startup is where "unresponsive" lives.
- **Every cancel window is wider than it looks.** Buffers are generous (8 f) and the buffer always stores the *latest* input.
- **Hitboxes are rectangles; hurtboxes are smaller than the sprite.** The player always gets the benefit of the doubt. Enemy hitboxes are smaller than their sprites; player attack hitboxes are larger than the blade.
- **The camera never fights the player.** 60 u horizontal lead in the facing direction (swinging at 4 u/f on a turn once Ash has gone the lead's whole swing, 120 u, that way or held the turn 20 f, never against the player's motion and never between touching a wall and next standing on the ground unless 400 u from that wall; across, the view moves with the player beyond the dead zone), dead zone of 40×30 u, vertical catches up at 0.08 lerp, never changing speed by more than 3 u/f per frame except to slow to the player's own speed when the player stops or turns, so it never outruns the player; look-down on crouch hold (200 u after 20 f of crouching with the stick held down, never during a strike). Vertically the camera rests on the last height the player stood, clung, kicked or bounced at, so jumps never move it: walking up continuous ground carries it at once, it eases up to a higher support at 8 u/f (pausing while the player falls away from it), follows a fall down at once, and rises with the player only beyond 256 u; while the player falls below the last support the view never rises, and while the player rises it never sinks. Falling, it looks 20 f of fall ahead along the fall's path, never past the ground it will land on or could still steer onto (a wall beside the fall is not ground), and the look ends the moment the fall does (it starts once the feet fall below the last support); dropping into a look-down, the looks together never turn the view up against the drop; dashing up, it looks 120 u ahead. The view stays within the room grown by the 80 u edge margin (128 u above and below, where the vertical follow eases in), easing into its edges. A teleport glides (24 f, at most 32 u/f), cutting an axis on which the old view would leave the player within 160 u of its edge, and finishing early (its speed changing by at most 5 u/f per frame) when the player presses on within 160 u of the edge it glides toward; a hazard respawn cuts. Cutscenes may take the camera; combat never does. Every value lives in `content/camera.tres` (ADR-0011).
- **Every surface tells you what it is at a glance.** Pogo-able things glow; wall-cling walls have a texture; non-cling walls are visibly smooth or wet.
- A debug overlay (F3) shows hitboxes, state, frame counters and input history on every build, forever.

## 6. Combat Specification

Combat is melee-first and directional. The player's blade (the Taper) strikes in four directions, every hit builds Flame, Flame pays for spells and healing, and every Votive is a proc that hangs off a hit. Enemies telegraph for at least 20 frames before anything that can hurt; the player's answer should take 2.

### The Taper (base attack)

| Strike | Startup | Active | Recovery | Damage | Knockback | Cancel into |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Side 1 | 2 f | 4 f | 8 f | 10 | 120 u/s away, player recoils 60 u/s for 8 f (8 u in all) | Side 2 from f 6, dash from f 4, jump any frame |
| Side 2 | 2 f | 4 f | 8 f | 10 | 140 u/s | Side 3 from f 6, dash from f 4 |
| Side 3 (finisher) | 3 f, armored | 5 f | 12 f | 16 | 260 u/s, launches light enemies | Dash from f 5 |
| Up | 2 f | 5 f | 9 f | 10 | 200 u/s up (juggle) | Dash from f 4, air attack any frame |
| Down (air only) | 2 f | 7 f (pogo hitbox f 3 to 9) | 4 f | 10 | Bounces player (section 5) | Anything on bounce; dash from f 4 (section 5: dash cancels any attack; docs/adr/0010) |
| Charged (hold 24 f, then let go) | 24 f charge, 3 f startup, armored | 6 f | 14 f | 36 | 400 u/s, breaks guards | Dash from f 8 |

- The chain resets if no attack input within 20 f of recovery ending.
- Armor: Side 3 and the Charged strike are armored through their 3 f of startup. A hit on those frames costs its hearts, Kindling tier and hitstop, but the strike is not interrupted and Ash is not knocked back (docs/adr/0010, docs/adr/0013). The rule of section 5 (more than 2 f of startup needs armor) is enforced on strikes by content validation; spells are exempt (docs/adr/0013).
- The side 1 recoil is a push of 60 u/s carried for 8 f after the hitstop, on top of the run; a dash, a cling, a hit, Mend, a death, a send-back or an ember-toss teleport ends it.
- Charged hold: only a hold whose press started a side strike arms the Charged strike. A hold from an up or down press (a pogo) never charges, and a death or a hazard send-back breaks a hold until the button is let go or pressed afresh.
- Attacking turns the player instantly; there is no turn-lock.
- Attack range: the hitbox extends 68 u from the player's center (sprite blade is about 50 u). Enemies feel reachable.
- Attack while running does not slow the player. Attack while dashing is a dash-slash.

### Flame (the resource)

- A bar of 100. Each Taper hit grants 11 Flame; each pogo 8; each parry 33; a kill's ember 11 when it reaches the player, 16 f after the kill. Spells cost 33. Mend costs 33 and heals 1 of the player's 5 base hearts over 50 frames of channeling (interruptible, the Flame is lost if interrupted).
- Flame drains at 1 per second when the player stands outside their Beacon's radius during a Vigil, and does not drain otherwise.
- Votives can raise the cap to 200, add passive regen, or make hits grant more.
- The design intent: aggression heals. The way to recover is to fight well, then spend the result.

### Kindling (the combo system)

Consecutive hits without taking damage build **Kindling**, shown as the blade visibly heating from dull iron to white.

| Tier | Hits | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ember | 0 to 7 | Baseline |
| Flame | 8 to 19 | +10% damage; hit sparks turn orange; Votive procs +10% chance |
| Blaze | 20 to 39 | +20% damage; attacks leave a 10 f fire trail that damages; finishers cause a small AoE |
| White | 40+ | +30% damage; dash has no cooldown; every 10th hit is an automatic Charged strike; the screen's ambient light rises slightly |

- Taking damage drops Kindling by one tier, not to zero. Dying resets it.
- Kindling is the visual spectacle of good play. The White tier is the state the trailer is cut from.

### Parry

- Press attack in the 8 f before an enemy's hit lands (any direction) to parry: the 8 frames that end on the frame it lands. The parry window is defined on the enemy's attack, not the player's input, so it is never a guess.
- A parry: 0 damage, 12 f of hitstop on both, enemy knocked back 200 u/s and staggered for 40 f, player grants 33 Flame and +5 Kindling.
- Parry is not a block. A failed parry is just an attack with a bad start. There is no block button.
- Bosses have attacks marked **unparryable** (a red flash instead of white) that must be dodged or pogoed.

### Spells (Ash's kit; other Wicks in section 4)

| Spell | Cost | Effect | Frames |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Kindle | 33 Flame | A cone of fire 180 u long in the stick direction; 30 damage; ignites | 4 f startup, 6 f active, 10 f recovery, cancelable from f 8 |
| Mend | 33 Flame | Heal 1 heart; channeled | 50 f, interruptible |
| Ember Toss | 0 Flame, 12 s cd | Throw the ember as a 600 u/s projectile that stops on walls; press again to teleport to it with 6 f of invulnerability | 3 f startup |
| Radiance (unlocked at Blaze+; spell with the stick down, on the ground) | 66 Flame | A burst that pushes all enemies 400 u/s away and lights the room fully for 5 s | 6 f startup |

### Hit feedback (the physics of satisfaction)

| Event | Hitstop | Screen shake | Knockback | Extras |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Light hit | 2 f both | 0 | Enemy flinch frame, white flash 2 f | Hit spark (3 variants), hit sound pitch randomized ±3% |
| Finisher | 4 f both | 1.5 u, 4 f | Launch | Slash arc smear, spark burst, bass thump |
| Charged | 6 f both | 3 u, 6 f | Big | Brief chromatic split 1 f, slow-motion 0.8× for 8 f on kill |
| Parry | 12 f both | 2 u, 6 f | Enemy only | Full-screen white flash 1 f, high chime, time to 0.5× for 6 f |
| Player hit | 8 f player only | 4 u, 8 f | 300 u/s away for 12 f (any action ends it), 20 f of invulnerability after the hitstop (docs/adr/0013) | Red vignette pulse, heart cracks, controller rumble 0.6 for 100 ms |
| Kill | 3 f | 1 u, 3 f | Corpse carries momentum | Enemy ember released as a particle that flies to the player (+Flame), chalk-ink dissolve |
| Boss phase | 20 f all | 6 u, 12 f |  | Music stinger, ambient light flips |

- Screen shake has a global scaler in settings and is capped at 6 u. Shake is directional (along the hit) not random.
- Hitstop is never applied to the player's own input reading: the player can buffer through it.
- A hazard (spikes) costs a heart as a hit does, and sends the player back to safe ground (docs/adr/0013).
- Every hit has a *weight*: damage above 30 adds 1 f of hitstop per 10 damage, capped at 8.

### Item procs

- Every Taper hit, pogo, spell tick and Dust scale is a **proc event** with a proc coefficient (Taper 1.0, pogo 0.8, Kindle 0.5 per tick, Dust 0.3 per scale, Radiance 0.2).
- Votives listen for events. A 10% proc Votive on a 0.5-coefficient hit is a 5% chance. Stacking adds chance linearly to a cap of 100%, then adds extra rolls.
- Proc chains are allowed (a proc can trigger another) to a depth of 3, then stop. This is what makes a late run look like fireworks without breaking the frame budget.

### Enemy-side rules

- Every enemy attack has a telegraph of ≥ 20 f with a distinct silhouette change and a sound.
- Every enemy has a **poise** value; player hits reduce it; at 0 the enemy staggers for 30 f and resets. This makes aggression safe against small enemies and a rhythm against large ones.
- No enemy has an attack that cannot be answered by at least two verbs (dash, pogo, parry, jump).
- Contact damage exists only for enemies that are visibly on fire, spiked or Snuffed-and-reaching. Walking into a wax-hound is not damage; its bite is.

## 7. Enemies & Bosses

Sixty-plus enemies across six zones, every one a verb the player has to answer with a verb of their own. An enemy earns its place by asking a movement question; a roster of damage sponges is a roster of nothing.

### Design philosophy

- **One question each.** A Soot-tick asks "can you pogo?" A Snuffed Knight asks "can you parry?" A Choir-mouth asks "can you close distance before the song lands?" Combinations of simple questions make hard rooms; no single enemy is a hard room.
- **Light-reactive.** Most Snuffed are drawn to the player's ember and to the Beacon. Some flee light; some are blinded by it. The player's light radius is a tool, not just a view.
- **Readable at a glance.** Silhouette first. Every enemy class has a distinct shape language: Snuffed are gaunt and upright, wax-things are round and dripping, Choir-things are long and ribbed, Root-things are angular and glowing.
- **Swarms scale, not stats.** The Wane Clock adds enemies and elite modifiers far more than it adds HP. A 30-minute room is crowded, not spongy.
- **Death is a gift.** Every kill releases an ember particle that flies to the player (Flame). Killing feels like harvesting light.

### Boss rules

- Every boss attack is answerable by at least two verbs; the "correct" answer is the stylish one, never the only one.
- Phase transitions are 20 f of hitstop, an ambient light change and a music stinger. The player is never hit during a transition.
- Bosses drop a **Relic** (a boss-tier Votive) and 3 to 5 regular Votives spread on the floor so the player picks a path through them.
- Boss HP bars are diegetic: the Gnawer's lantern-growths, the Abbess's cloak burning away, the Cantor's voice fading. A traditional bar is available in settings.

## 8. Votives & the Wane Clock

Votives are stackable passive items: 120 at launch, four tiers, every one a proc or a modifier that changes what hits *do*. Stacking is the whole point; a run that ends with 40 Votives should look like a different game than it started as, and feel like the same one.

### Tiers

| Tier | Count | Drop share | Flavor | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Candle** (common) | 55 | 65% | Small, honest, stack forever | *Pilgrim's Wick*: +8% run speed per stack. *Tallow Knuckle*: hits deal +3 flat damage per stack. |
| **Lantern** (uncommon) | 35 | 25% | Build-defining procs | *Bell of the Vigil*: 10% chance on hit to ring, stunning everything in 120 u; stacks add chance. *Second Ember*: pogo bounces grant 1 extra air dash per stack. |
| **Star** (rare) | 20 | 8% | Run-changing | *Hearthshard*: your ember's light radius doubles and enemies inside it take 15% more damage; stacks add 50% radius. *The Abbess's Mercy*: parries heal half a heart; stacks add a quarter. |
| **Relic** (boss) | 12 | Boss drops | Unique to a boss, one per run usually | *Gnawer's Lantern*: Radiance costs 0 for 1 use per zone. *Warden's Lantern*: on death, relight once per run at 1 heart with White Kindling. |
| **Soot** (corruption) | 15 | Lamplighter Shrine only | Strong, with a cost | *Snuffed Blade*: +40% damage; the Wane Clock runs 20% faster. *Hollow Step*: dash has no cooldown; max hearts −1 per stack. |

### Stacking math

- **Linear**: flat damage, run speed, radius. Add per stack, no cap. These are the Candles.
- **Chance**: proc chance adds linearly to 100%, then each further stack is an extra roll. A 10%-chance Lantern at 10 stacks procs every hit; at 15 stacks, every hit plus a 50% chance of a second.
- **Hyperbolic** for defensive stats (armor, damage reduction): `1 − 1 / (1 + 0.1 × stacks)`, so 10 stacks is 50%, never 100%.
- **Diminishing** only where a hard cap would feel bad (cooldown reduction: each stack removes 15% of what remains).
- A tooltip shows the live number with stacks counted. No hidden math.

### Vessels (active items)

One Vessel slot, bound to the fourth face button. Vessels are charged by kills (10 to 30) rather than time. Examples: *Chandler's Flask* (restore 2 hearts), *Choir-horn* (pull every enemy on screen to you), *Lamplighter's Match* (set your light radius to maximum for 20 s and ignite everything in it), *The Hourglass* (freeze the Wane Clock for 60 s; 60 kills to charge).

### Tallow (currency)

- Dropped by enemies, scales with the clock so a late-run kill pays more. Spent on Tapers (chests: 25 at minute 0, scaling), Shrines (gamble: 40% Candle, 35% nothing, 20% Lantern, 5% Star), and the Chandler's wares in the Warrens.
- Tallow does not carry between runs. Nothing carries between runs except unlocks (section 9).

### The Wane Clock

The clock starts at 0:00 and never stops. It drives every scaling number in the run through one difficulty coefficient:

```latex
D(t) = 1 + 0.18 \cdot \sqrt{t_{min}} + 0.012 \cdot t_{min} + 0.35 \cdot (zone - 1)
```

where `t_min` is elapsed minutes and `zone` is the current depth. At minute 0 in zone 1, D is 1.0; at minute 20 in zone 3, D is about 2.75; at minute 45 in zone 5, D is about 3.95.

| What D drives | Formula | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Enemy HP | `base × D` | Gentle |
| Enemy damage | `base × (0.6 + 0.4 × D)` | Damage scales slower than HP; the player should die to being overwhelmed, not one-shot |
| Spawn count per room | `base × D^1.3` | The main pressure |
| Elite chance | `min(60%, 4% × (D − 1) × 10)` | Elites appear at about minute 8 |
| Ambient light | `base × max(0.15, 1 − 0.09 × (D − 1))` | The world literally darkens; by D = 4 only the player's ember and enemy eyes are visible |
| Tallow drops | `base × D` | Greed pays, but spawns pay back |
| Vigil length | `90 + 12 × (zone − 1)` seconds | Not clock-scaled, so lingering is punished by spawns, not by longer holds |

### The Dark

The ambient darkening is the clock made visible. Named thresholds the player will learn by sight:

- **Dusk** (D < 1.6): normal; the sky and backgrounds are legible.
- **Gloam** (1.6 to 2.6): backgrounds fade to silhouette; enemies' eyes begin to glow; the music gains a low drone.
- **Pitch** (2.6 to 3.6): only the ember and Beacons cast light; parallax backgrounds are near black; Clingers may appear in any zone.
- **The Hush Breathes** (> 3.6): the screen edges pulse; a heartbeat in the mix; the Remembered can appear; Soot Votives cost nothing at Shrines.

The Dark is never a hard fail. A player who likes it can live in it. They will just be fighting the whole world by hand.

## 9. The Hearth & Meta-progression

Nothing carries between runs except knowledge, unlocks and the people you have saved. There is no permanent stat upgrade in WANE. The player gets better; the Wick does not.

### The Hearth (hub)

The cold temple at the top of the world where the Vigil once kept the sun's last altar. Small: one screen wide, three tall. It fills as the player progresses.

- **The Altar**: start a run. Choose a Wick, a difficulty, and (once unlocked) a Soot Pact.
- **The Lamplighter's Desk**: read found notes; the lore is reassembled here as a growing manuscript.
- **The Logbook**: every enemy, boss, Votive and Wick, with discovery state, the in-world line and the numbers. Entries unlock on first encounter and fill in on first kill.
- **The Rescued**: NPCs saved in runs appear here. The Chandler sells cosmetics for Tallow found in the hub only (a small amount per run). The Knight-Abbess trains (practice any boss). The Cantor hums (music player).
- **The Dark Mirror**: fight the Remembered versions of your past Wicks; a boss rush.
- **The Hearth itself**: unlit at first. Each ending lights one of three braziers.

- 120 Challenges, one per Votive, each a sentence long, each teaching something about the systems.

- The game must be beatable with the starting Wick, the starting 40 Votives and no unlocks, by a skilled player, on Wane difficulty. This is tested every milestone.

- If a Votive is unlocked and makes the game easier on average, it is balanced, not gated.

## 10. Art Direction

Ink and gouache, authored entirely in code by Claude, lit by one real light. The look is a painted storybook that someone is holding a candle up to. Nothing on screen is stock, downloaded or generated by a raster image model: every shape is a vector or a procedure, every line and fill is a shader, so the art is consistent, editable, deterministic and resolution-independent. That constraint is the style: silhouettes and light carry everything, and the world is beautiful because of what the ember reveals, not because of painted detail.

### Style

- **Silhouette first.** Every character, enemy and prop is a small set of closed vector shapes designed to read at 64 px. A shape that needs interior detail to be understood is redesigned, not decorated.
- **Ink line as a shader.** Outlines are never drawn. They are computed from each shape's signed-distance field: weight driven by the light direction (thick on the shadow side, hairline on the lit side), a two-octave noise wobble along the edge so no two frames of line are identical, and a pressure taper at shape ends. Characters get a closed line; backgrounds get an open, broken line that drops out where light hits.
- **Gouache fill as a shader.** Two flat values per surface (lit and shadow) plus a rim, with a procedural brush texture (directional streaks aligned to the shape's major axis, tiled noise, slight edge darkening where fill meets line) and screen-space paper grain. No gradients in source; all gradation comes from the lighting pass.
- **Shape language**: Wicks are tall and narrow with large heads and small hands. Snuffed are Wicks with the warmth removed: same proportions, slack posture, no eyes, only the socket. Wax-things are circles and drips. Choir-things are ribbed verticals. Root-things are angular with internal glow.
- **The ember**: a single orange-white point in every Wick's chest, a soft bloom over the ink. It is the player's cursor, their health, their light and the game's logo.
- **Mood references** (studied from memory for feeling, never sampled, traced or reproduced): heavy blacks, storybook gouache, a candlelit painting's falloff. The art must stand on its own silhouettes and light.

### Lighting (the thematic core)

The engine draws the world dark and the player lights it. Lighting is a true 2D pass, not painted into sprites.

- **Every sprite ships with a normal map and an emissive map**, painted (not generated) for hero assets, generated with artist correction for the rest. Normal maps let the ember rake across a knight's armor as the player walks past.
- **Light sources**: the player's ember (radius 420 u at base, warm 2400 K, flicker at 0.5 Hz with ±4% amplitude), Beacons (cool white, huge), enemy eyes (point lights, tiny), fire (orange, animated), the Ember in the Gutter (red, the only light that casts up).
- **Shadows**: 2D ray-marched soft shadows from the ember against the collision layer. Shadows are the game's depth cue and its horror cue: a Clinger is a shadow before it is a sprite.
- **Ambient**: a per-zone ambient color multiplied by the Wane Clock's darkness factor (section 8). At Pitch, ambient is 0.15 and the world is the ember.
- **Light is information**: pogo-able surfaces are emissive; wax softens as emissive intensity rises near the ember; a parry window is a white flash; the unparryable is red.

### Animation

- **Rigs and poses as data.** Each character is an SVG part library (head, thorax, cloak, limbs, blade, each with a pivot) plus a rig (bones, constraints, foot and hand IK). An action is 4 to 8 key poses with timing in a `.pose.tres`: bone rotations, shape deformations, a smear flag per bone. Claude authors the poses the way an animator would draw them, as strong silhouettes at the extremes.
- **Generated in-betweens, generated smears.** The animator eases per bone between keys, derives squash on landing (3 f, 110% wide) and stretch at jump apex (2 f, 94% wide) from vertical velocity, and generates smears from per-bone velocity: a fast blade is stretched along its motion arc and its ink line becomes a motion ribbon. A linear tween between two still poses is forbidden; every fast move carries a smear.
- **Baked, then alive.** Actions are rendered offline at 4× to sprite sheets (24 fps for Wicks, 12 to 24 fps for enemies by size), with normal maps derived from the shape SDF bevel and emissive masks from tagged parts. Cloaks, antennae, wax drips and chain links run on a lightweight 2D verlet system at runtime over the baked body.
- **Rules**: anticipation on every enemy attack is ≥ 20 f and a silhouette change, not a flash. Player anticipation is ≤ 2 f. Recovery frames are where the pose is beautiful; that is where a screenshot lands.
- **Claude looks at every animation.** Each action renders a contact sheet; Claude opens it, critiques arcs, spacing, silhouette and smear against these rules, and re-poses. Hero actions (Signatures, boss phases) get at least three passes.

### VFX language

- Hit sparks are ink splatters with a hot core, 3 to 5 variants per hit type, randomly rotated and scaled ±15%.
- Dust on landing and direction change: 3 f puffs.
- Dash: a 4-ghost trail of the player's ink silhouette fading over 8 f, plus a motion-blur streak of the ember.
- Kindling tiers change the blade's emissive from none to white-hot and add a trail.
- Death: the enemy's ink dissolves (a dissolve shader on the line layer), the fill falls as ash, the ember rises.
- Every proc has a **short, distinct, sub-10-frame** VFX. With 40 procs firing, the screen must still be readable; the rule is one bright thing per 100×100 u region per 3 frames, enforced by a VFX budget manager that culls the least important.

### Backgrounds and parallax

- 5 to 7 parallax layers per zone at 0.1, 0.25, 0.4, 0.6, 0.8, 1.0 and 1.2 (foreground) scroll factors. Far layers render flat with atmospheric perspective; near layers receive the ember's light and carry normal maps.
- Layers are built from parametric generators per zone (candle-stump towers, wax drips and sags from noise, cathedral vaults and ribs from arch parameters, root veins from L-systems, water caustics and buoys, sun-tree branches) composed per room by a composition script, so every room is unique and every zone is one material language.
- Each zone has 3 to 4 vistas: rooms built for a single composition where the camera pulls back and the player is small against the world. Vistas are composed individually, rendered, critiqued and recomposed until the frame could be the store page.
- Tilesets are autotiles generated from a material definition (ash, wax, brass, bone, water, root) with edge rules and 4 variants per tile so repetition never shows.
- Foreground elements (fronds, chains, drips) occlude the player briefly and never during combat-critical frames (foreground fades to 30% alpha when the player is within 60 u).

### Pipeline

- **Sources** live in `assets/art/src/`: SVG part libraries, rigs, poses, material definitions, generator parameters, room compositions, palettes. Sources are the art; renders are build output.
- **Build**: `tools/artbuild/` renders sources through the ink and gouache shaders at 4× native (4K-ready from day one), downsamples to 1080p native with a nearest-plus-bicubic hybrid that keeps the line crisp, packs sprite sheets with metadata JSON (hitboxes, hurtboxes, pogo boxes, emissive regions, smear flags) and writes normal and emissive maps. Noise is seeded, so builds are deterministic and screenshot regression works.
- **Fonts** are open-licensed only (SIL OFL or equivalent), listed in `docs/LICENSES.md`; nothing else from outside the repo enters the art.
- **Review**: every asset renders at three sizes (64 px silhouette, 1× in a lit test room at Dusk and Pitch, 4× detail) plus a contact sheet per animation. Claude opens the images and critiques them before a reviewer subagent that did not author them does the same. No asset ships unseen.
- **Style gate**: automated checks (silhouette contrast at 64 px, line-weight range, two-value separation, emissive mask present, reads at Pitch ambient) plus the visual critique. Hero assets (Wicks, bosses, vistas) get at least three iterations; an asset that fails after three is redesigned from the silhouette up, not patched.
- **Gallery**: every build regenerates `docs/gallery/index.html` with every asset, animation and palette, so the director reviews the whole game's art on one page.

## 11. Audio Direction

The score is a choir that has forgotten the words, and the sound design is a candle in a stone room. Audio is the second light: the player should be able to close their eyes and know where the enemies are.

### Score

- **Instrumentation**: solo cello, a small vocal ensemble (6 voices, wordless), prepared piano, glass harmonica, and a low analogue drone. No percussion until zone 5; the Cinder Root introduces a single tuned drum that the Root-veins erupt on.
- **Structure**: each zone has a 3-layer adaptive cue (ambient, tension, combat) crossfaded by the number of enemies aware of the player and the Wane Clock's Dark threshold. Gloam adds the drone; Pitch removes the cello and leaves the voices; the Hush Breathes adds a heartbeat at 54 bpm.
- **Leitmotifs**: the Hearth (a rising fourth), the Wicks (the Hearth motif inverted), the Hush (silence; its "theme" is the absence of the drone, which the ear notices), the Choir (a true chord that is only heard complete in the Lantern ending).
- **Boss cues** are through-composed with phase transitions on musical boundaries; the game waits up to 2 bars for the stinger to land on a downbeat.
- **The Cantor** fight is rhythm-synced: the arena's attacks land on beats, the parry window is a musical anticipation, and the player's hits play notes in the key.

### Sound design

- **The Taper**: three layered sounds per hit (a cloth-tearing transient, a tonal ring that pitches up with Kindling tier, a sub thump scaled by damage). Finishers add a bell.
- **Movement**: footsteps by surface (ash, wax, brass, bone, water, root), dash is an exhale plus a candle-gutter, pogo is a struck bell with pitch rising on each chain hit (resets after 1 s), wall-cling is a cloth scrape.
- **The ember**: a constant soft crackle whose volume follows the player's health and whose pitch follows Kindling. It is the player's heartbeat and the mix's anchor.
- **Enemies**: every telegraph has a sound 20 f before the hit, panned to position. Snuffed are silent except for their attacks; wax-things drip; Choir-things sing their attacks as tones (the ring you see is the note you hear).
- **Parry**: a single high chime with the mix ducked for 6 f. It is the best sound in the game.
- **Darkness**: at Pitch, the mix gains reverb and loses high end; the player hears the room they cannot see.

### Mix rules

- Player sounds always cut through; enemy sounds are side-chained to the player's attacks by 3 dB.
- Hard cap of 24 simultaneous voices; a priority system drops the furthest, quietest sound first.
- Everything the player does has a sound; nothing the UI does has a sound louder than −18 dB.
- No sound repeats identically: ±3% pitch and 3 to 5 variants on everything that plays more than once a second.

### Tools

Every sound and every note is synthesized by Claude in code; no samples, no sample libraries, no middleware. `tools/audiobuild/` renders stems and effects to OGG from synth definitions: a bowed-string model for the cello (Karplus-Strong with bow excitation and body resonance), formant synthesis for the wordless choir, modal synthesis plus noise for the prepared piano, FM and additive for the glass harmonica, detuned saws through slow filters for the drone, and convolution reverb from synthesized impulse responses sized to each zone. The score is data (`.score.tres`: key, tempo, layers, bars, motifs) rendered to one stem per adaptive layer, so a cue can be rewritten by editing bars, not audio. At runtime a pure-GDScript adaptive engine does bar-aligned layer crossfades, side-chain ducking, the 24-voice priority system, pitch and variant randomization and the Dark's reverb and filter changes. Claude checks what it cannot hear: every render gets a spectrogram, a loudness plot and a per-event timing check against the mix rules above, and the director listens for the rest. Music is authored in the project's key per zone so in-world tonal sounds are always consonant with the score.

## 13. Technical Architecture

Godot 4.x, with a deterministic simulation core that owns every gameplay rule and a Godot scene tree that only draws it. The simulation runs at a fixed 60 Hz on integer sub-pixel positions, so a run is a seed plus an input stream, and every movement tech is a replay test in CI.

&#91;embedded content: architecture · input, deterministic core, presentation, content, tooling\]

Input feeds the core once per tick; the core hands a state snapshot to the Godot view; content defines the core's numbers; tooling records the seed and inputs and replays them in CI.

### Engine choice

| Option | Verdict | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Godot 4.x (chosen)** | Yes | Open source with full engine source for hot-path fixes; no royalty; scenes and resources are text, so an agent can author everything without an editor; built-in 2D normal-mapped lighting and occluders as a base to extend; GDExtension (C++) for hot paths; headless mode for CI; ships to every target platform |
| Unity | No | Closed engine, licensing risk, binary scenes fight agentic authoring |
| Unreal | No | 2D is a second-class citizen; cost of the engine's weight for no gain |
| Custom engine (Rust/Bevy) | No, revisit if Godot's lighting hits a wall | Total control over feel, but a year of engine work before the first room; Godot's text pipeline gets 90% of the control for 10% of the cost |
| GameMaker | No | Closed, weak lighting, weak tooling for the pipeline described in section 10 |

Language split: **GDScript (static typing enforced)** for gameplay, content and tools; **C++ via GDExtension** for the physics sweep, hitbox resolution, the VFX budget manager and the lighting pass once profiling demands it; **GLSL** for shaders. Godot version pinned per milestone; upgrades are a tracked task with a full regression run.

### Layers

1. **Input**: polls raw device state every frame before physics into an `InputFrame` (buttons as a bitfield, stick as fixed-point) and keeps an 8-frame ring buffer that always serves the latest press. Remapping and dead zones live here and nowhere else.
2. **Simulation core** (`core/`): pure GDScript and C++ with no Node dependency. Owns entities, the AABB physics sweep (slopes, one-way platforms, corner correction), the player and enemy state machines, hitbox and hurtbox resolution, the proc event bus, Votive stacking, the Wane Clock, room and zone state, and a seeded RNG with split streams (loot, spawns, VFX, AI). Positions are integers in 1/256-unit sub-pixels. The core is stepped exactly once per 60 Hz tick and never touches rendering, audio or Godot's physics server.
3. **Content** (`content/`): every Wick, Votive, enemy, boss, room and zone is a text resource with numbers, references and hooks. Designers tune without code; a Votive is a resource with a proc listener script and a stacking curve.
4. **Presentation** (`game/`): the Godot scene tree as a view. Frame-indexed sprite animators driven by the core's state, the lighting pass (normal maps, ember point light with soft shadows, emissives, ambient × Dark factor), the VFX budget manager, the camera, the pure-GDScript adaptive audio engine, and UI. Rendering interpolates between the last two simulation states for high refresh rates.
5. **Services**: profile and save (atomic writes, 3 rotating backups), seeded daily runs, leaderboards, the replay recorder (seed plus input stream plus periodic state hashes), opt-in telemetry (deaths by room, time per zone, Votive pick rates), localization.
6. **Tooling**: the F3 debug overlay, a frame-stepper, a replay viewer with scrubbing, a room editor as a Godot editor plugin, the sprite-sheet importer that reads the artist's hitbox metadata, the art build (vector and procedural sources to sprite sheets, normal and emissive maps), the audio build (synth definitions and score data to stems), the style-gate linter, the asset gallery, and CI.

```
  content/     wicks/ votives/ vessels/ enemies/ bosses/ rooms/ zones/ notes/

```

### Determinism and replays

- One `step(input: InputFrame)` per tick; no gameplay in `_process`. Floating point is allowed only in presentation.
- Every run records seed, Wick, difficulty, pacts and the input stream (about 2 KB per minute). A replay reproduces the run bit-for-bit on any platform; a desync is a P0 bug caught by a state hash every 60 ticks.
- All 12 tech interactions and every boss phase are replay fixtures in `tests/replay/`: an input script and the expected state at named frames. They run headless in under 10 seconds total.

## 15. The Immaculate Checklist

Every milestone build passes every line below before any new feature is started. A line that fails is the only task on the board until it passes. Items are checked by playing, with the F3 overlay on, by someone who did not write the code.

### Movement

- [ ] Jump pressed 8 frames before landing fires on the landing frame
- [ ] Jump pressed 6 frames after walking off a ledge still jumps
- [ ] Tap jump and full jump differ by at least 3 tiles and the release feels like a decision, not a cut
- [ ] Turning around never produces a dead frame; the sprite flips on the input frame
- [ ] Dash from standstill and dash at full run reach the same distance
- [ ] Dash-jump keeps 100% of dash speed and clears the reference gap in zone 1 room 7 every time
- [ ] Pogo keeps full horizontal speed; a 10-bounce chain across the zone 1 spike pit is possible at 4 bounces per second
- [ ] Pogo resets air dash; pogo, dash, pogo crosses the reference hidden-room gap
- [ ] Wall-kick climbs a single wall without the player ever feeling stuck to it
- [ ] Landing on a 1-pixel ledge edge lands; the corner-correction nudge is invisible
- [ ] Head bumping a ceiling corner by under 6 units slides around it
- [ ] The camera never shows the player within 80 units of the screen edge during normal play
- [ ] A 60-second movement-only loop through zone 1 can be played with eyes on the character, never on the HUD

### Combat

- [ ] Attack comes out on frame 3 from a neutral press, every time, including during hitstop
- [ ] Dash cancels an attack on frame 4 and the cancel never eats the next attack input
- [ ] A 3-hit chain against a Tallowman reads as three distinct impacts with rising weight
- [ ] Parry succeeds on the 8-frame window against the Snuffed Knight 10 times in a row for a skilled tester
- [ ] The unparryable red attack is distinguishable from the parryable white one at Pitch ambient with flashes set to 0
- [ ] Taking a hit is never confusing: the tester can say what hit them without a replay
- [ ] Hitstop never causes a dropped input; buffered inputs fire on the first free frame
- [ ] With 30 Votives active, the screen stays readable: the player and every enemy telegraph are visible
- [ ] Kindling White is reached by a skilled tester in zone 2 and feels earned, not inevitable

- [ ] At Pitch ambient, the ember's light reveals a Clinger 30 frames before it drops

- [ ] Every attack has a smear frame; no attack animation is a tween between two still poses
- [ ] Landing squash and jump stretch are visible and do not block any input

- [ ] The Dark thresholds are distinguishable by sight alone

- [ ] A seeded replay of a full run reproduces bit-for-bit on Windows, Linux and the Deck

### Process

- [ ] Every new system has an ADR

```markdown
- Everything runs headless: `godot --headless --path . --script tools/ci/run_tests.gd`; exports with `godot --headless --export-release`.
- gdUnit4 for tests. gdlint and gdformat in CI with zero warnings.
- Python 3 with numpy, cairosvg (or resvg) and Pillow for the art and audio builds under `tools/`. Pin versions in `tools/requirements.txt`.

## Non-negotiable architecture rules

1. The simulation core (`core/`) has no Node dependency, runs at a fixed 60 Hz, one `step(input)` per tick, and never touches rendering, audio or Godot physics.
2. Positions and velocities in the core are integers in 1/256-unit sub-pixels. Floats exist only in `game/` (presentation).
3. Input is read every tick before physics into an `InputFrame`; an 8-frame ring buffer serves the latest press. No input ever waits for an animation.
4. A run is a seed plus an input stream. The replay recorder is built in Phase 1, before the first enemy. A desync is a P0 bug.
5. Animations serve the state machine, never the reverse. A 9-frame move is 9 frames; the animator renders it with generated smears and holds.
6. Every number a designer might tune lives in `content/` as a text resource, never as a literal in code. The feel log records every change.
7. Nothing is drawn without the VFX budget manager and nothing is heard without the audio priority system.
8. Hitboxes are rectangles; player hurtboxes are smaller than the sprite; player attack boxes are larger than the blade.
9. The camera never takes control during combat. Lead, dead zone and lerp values come from `content/camera.tres`.
10. Nothing enters the repo from outside it: no asset packs, no downloaded textures or sounds, no sample libraries, no raster image generators, no fonts outside open licenses listed in `docs/LICENSES.md`. Every visual and audible thing is authored from source.

## Working method

Follow the roadmap in BUILD.md section 14, phase by phase, in order. Do not start Phase 2 work while any Phase 1 line of the Immaculate Checklist fails.

For every task:

1. Read the relevant BUILD.md section and quote the numbers you are implementing in the commit description.
2. Write the test or replay fixture first. For movement and combat that means an input script in `tests/replay/` and the expected state at named frames. For an asset it means the style-gate check it must pass.
3. Implement.
4. Run the whole suite headless. Run the stress-room perf benchmark if you touched `core/`, `game/vfx/` or `game/lighting/`.
5. Look at it. For gameplay: run `tools/replay/play.gd` on the fixture with the F3 overlay on and save a frame-stepped contact sheet to `docs/feel_log/<date>-<task>.png`. For art: render the asset at 64 px, 1x in a lit test room at Dusk and at Pitch, and 4x, plus a contact sheet per animation. Open every image and critique it in writing against BUILD.md section 10. For audio: render a spectrogram and loudness plot and check them against section 11.
6. Spawn a reviewer subagent that has not seen your work, with this prompt: "Review this change against BUILD.md sections X and Y and the Immaculate Checklist. Try to break the feel. For art, open the renders and judge silhouette, line, value separation, readability at Pitch, arcs and smears. Report every frame where an input is late, a cancel is eaten, a hitbox disagrees with the doc, a number differs from the spec, or an asset would not pass the style gate." Fix everything it finds. Repeat until it finds nothing.
7. Write the feel log entry (before, after, why) and an ADR if you made a non-obvious choice.
8. Commit with a message that names the BUILD.md section and the checklist lines affected.

Definition of done: tests green, replay fixture green, perf gate green, style gate green for any asset, feel log entry written, reviewer subagent satisfied, PROGRESS.md and the asset gallery updated.

## Art: you make all of it

There are no artists on this project. You are the art director and the animator, and the bar is BUILD.md section 10. Working rules:

- Sources are the art. Every character, enemy, prop, tile, background layer and UI element is a vector part library, a rig, a pose file, a material definition or a generator with parameters under `assets/art/src/`. Renders are build output from `tools/artbuild/` and are never edited by hand.
- Silhouette first. Design every shape at 64 px before adding anything. If it does not read as what it is at 64 px against a black background, redesign it; do not decorate it.
- Line and fill are shaders. The ink line is computed from each shape's signed-distance field with light-driven weight and seeded noise wobble; the gouache fill is two values plus rim with procedural brush texture and paper grain. Build these shaders first, in Phase 2 task 1, and run every asset through them; never paint a line or a texture into a source.
- Animation is key poses as data. Author 4 to 8 strong extremes per action with timing; the animator eases per bone, derives squash and stretch from velocity and generates smears from per-bone velocity. A linear tween between two still poses is forbidden. Bake Wicks at 24 fps, enemies at 12 to 24 by size, with normal maps from the SDF bevel and emissive masks from tagged parts.
- Backgrounds are parametric generators composed per room: candle-stump towers, wax sags, cathedral vaults, L-system roots, water caustics, sun-tree branches. Every zone is one material language; every room is a unique composition. Vistas (3 to 4 per zone) are composed individually and iterated until the frame could be the store page.
- Look at everything. Open every render you produce. Critique it in writing against the style, animation and lighting rules before the reviewer does. No asset ships unseen. Hero assets (Wicks, bosses, vistas, Signatures) get at least three full iterations; an asset that still fails after three is redesigned from the silhouette up, not patched.
- Light does the work. Lighting (section 10) is what makes this art direction possible: build the normal-mapped ember light, soft shadows, emissives and the ambient-times-Dark factor before the first final asset, and judge every asset lit, at Dusk and at Pitch, never flat.
- Regenerate `docs/gallery/index.html` on every build: every asset, every animation, every palette, every vista, with the style-gate results beside them. That page is the director's art review.
- Mood references are studied from memory for feeling only. Nothing is traced, sampled, or made recognizable as any existing game's or artist's work.

## Audio: you make all of it

There is no composer and no sound designer. You synthesize everything, and the bar is BUILD.md section 11.

- `tools/audiobuild/` renders every sound and every score stem to OGG from synth definitions: a bowed-string model for the cello, formant synthesis for the wordless choir, modal synthesis for the prepared piano, FM and additive for the glass harmonica, detuned saws through slow filters for the drone, and convolution reverb from synthesized impulse responses sized per zone. No samples, no sample libraries, no middleware.
- The score is data: `.score.tres` files with key, tempo, motifs, layers and bars, rendered to one stem per adaptive layer. Rewrite a cue by editing bars, not audio. Leitmotifs, zone keys and the Cantor's beat-synced fight follow section 11.
- Sound effects follow the layering rule: transient plus tone plus sub for every hit, surface-specific footsteps, the ember crackle tied to health and Kindling, and 3 to 5 variants with pitch randomization for anything that plays more than once a second.
- Runtime is a pure-GDScript adaptive engine: bar-aligned crossfades between ambient, tension and combat layers, side-chain ducking, the 24-voice priority system, and the Dark's reverb and filter changes.
- Check what you cannot hear: spectrogram, loudness over time, event timing (a telegraph sound exactly 20 frames before its hit), and a mix report per build. Flag anything you are unsure of for the director to listen to, and say what to listen for.

## Content

- Every Wick, Votive, Vessel, enemy, boss, room and zone is a `.tres` resource under `content/` with a schema validated in CI. A Votive is a resource with a proc listener and a stacking curve; adding one never touches `core/`.
- Rooms are authored in the room editor plugin (`tools/room_editor/`) and stored as text. Build the editor in Phase 1, task 7.

## Feel: how to tune

- Start from the numbers in BUILD.md sections 5 and 6. They are starting values; the tuned values become the spec, and you update BUILD.md when they change.
- Tune one number at a time. Record it. Play the fixture. Compare contact sheets.
- Prefer removing a frame of startup over adding a frame of generosity. Prefer a wider cancel window over a faster animation.
- If a feature cannot be made to feel right in two tuning passes, write it up in PROGRESS.md and propose cutting it. Never ship it mushy.

## Prohibited

- Gameplay logic in `_process` or `_physics_process`. The core is stepped by the game loop only.
- Float positions in `core/`. Godot physics bodies for the player or enemies.
- Literals for tunable numbers. Silenced warnings. Skipped, disabled or flaky tests. Lowering a gate threshold.
- "Temporary" code in `core/`. If it must exist, it lives behind a feature flag with an expiry date in PROGRESS.md.
- Placeholder art or audio in any build after Phase 1. Grey boxes are for Phase 1 only, and they are never described as art.
- Hand-edited render output, stock assets, downloaded textures or sounds, sample libraries, raster image generators, unlicensed fonts.
- Claiming done without the reviewer subagent's sign-off, or shipping an asset you have not looked at.
- Copying mechanics, names, creatures, art or music from Hollow Knight, Risk of Rain or any other game. The references are studied; nothing is reproduced.

## Communication

- Keep PROGRESS.md current: phase, status of every checklist line, what is next, what is blocked, and what the director should look at or listen to this milestone.
- Ask the human only for decisions BUILD.md leaves open and an ADR cannot settle. Otherwise decide, record the ADR, move on.
- Report in plain language. Lead with what the player can now do, see or hear that they could not before.
```
